# Circuit research — ULT-SOUND DS-4 / DS-4M

> Goal: ground the Phase 2 DSP redesign in the *actual* analog circuit
> of the instrument rather than guessing from the panel.

## TL;DR — what we obtained, what we did not

Because the DS-4 is a Japanese 1978 derivative of the Pollard Syndrum
family (acknowledged in the modern reissue's marketing copy), the
Pollard schematic is the most reliable *public* reference for the
circuit topology. Where the DS-4 panel adds features that the Syndrum
doesn't have (MULTI VCO PULL, BEAT SENSE, OCTAVE 1-5, the LFO
section, the per-channel CYMBAL/SNARE/NOISE label, etc.) we infer the
additional circuit blocks from the panel control set and from
contemporary Japanese drum-synth practice.

The key public-domain fact, from Clacktronics' Pollard Syndrum DIY
clone and the search-result quote of the original service manual, is
that the **trigger is conditioned then routed to two envelope
generators — one for amplitude, one for pitch sweep** — and that the
**VCO is an ICL8038 function-generator IC** (a single 14-pin chip that
emits sine, triangle and square from one timing capacitor).

## 2. The ICL8038 (and what it means for our DSP)

The Intersil ICL8038 is a *triangle-core* function generator. It is
not a Curtis CEM3340-style exponential VCO; it is a current-controlled
ramp oscillator. The internals are:

1. Two matched current sources, one sourcing, one sinking; their
   magnitudes are equal and proportional to the CV pin.
2. A timing capacitor `Ct`. When the upper threshold is hit, the
   internal flip-flop flips and the second current source is enabled
   to discharge `Ct`; symmetric at the lower threshold.
3. The voltage on `Ct` is therefore an exact triangle, with frequency
   `f = I / (Ct · V_swing)`.
4. A comparator on the flip-flop output gives the square wave.
5. The sine wave is *not* generated by an integrator — it is the
   triangle passed through a *piecewise-linear breakpoint shaper*
   (typically 7 diode/resistor segments per polarity, configured to
   approximate `sin(π/2 · tri)` to ≈1 % THD).

Why this matters for the model:

* "Sine" on the DS-4 panel is therefore not a pure mathematical sine.
  It is a piecewise-linear approximation with ~1 % THD; the residual
  harmonics are odd (3rd ≈ -42 dBc, 5th ≈ -55 dBc) and they are part
  of the instrument's character. A `std::sin()` call is too clean.
* "Triangle" output (which the DS-4 doesn't expose — see §3) is the
  actual capacitor voltage; it has no aliasing source because it is a
  continuous ramp.
* "Square" is hard-switching and full-band; this is where PolyBLEP is
  needed.
* The CV → frequency relationship is **linear, not exponential**.
  Doubling the current doubles the frequency. So OCTAVE 1..5 on the
  panel is implemented as a **5-step current divider / multiplier
  network**, *not* a 1V/octave keyboard converter.
* The pitch sweep envelope is summed into the CV pin as a *current*,
  not a voltage — meaning the sweep amount is multiplicative on the
  base frequency, which matches the panel's "0–6 OCT" SWEEP WIDTH
  scale exactly.

## 3. DS-4 panel control ↔ inferred circuit block

| Panel control                  | Likely circuit block                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **TUNING** (note-ring knob)    | Coarse pot setting the ICL8038 control current. The C..B silk-screen is decorative — there's no key tracking, only a frequency setting.                                                               |
| **BEAT TUNE** (vertical fader) | Series pot adding a small offset current. Produces a few-Hz beating against the main VCO when MULTI VCO is engaged.                                                                                    |
| **OCTAVE 1-5** slide switch    | 5-position resistor ladder × the control current. 1 = ÷4, 3 = ×1, 5 = ×4 (typical 5-step factor of 2 ladder).                                                                                          |
| **ATTACK** rotary              | Series resistor in the *attack* charge path of the amp envelope (charges Ca through R_attack from the trigger pulse).                                                                                  |
| **SUSTAIN** fader              | Series resistor in the *decay* path of the amp envelope (discharges Ca through R_sustain to ground).                                                                                                   |
| **CYMBAL / SNARE / NOISE** pill at the bottom of the ATTACK column | Marks the **per-channel noise voicing** — silk-screen identifier, not a switch. The actual character is set by fixed resistors/caps in the noise bandpass for that channel.                                            |
| The little switch under it     | Selects between **pure VCO** and **VCO + Noise mix** (i.e., enables/disables the noise gate).                                                                                                          |
| **LFO LEVEL** rotary           | Pot scaling the LFO output before it sums into the VCO CV.                                                                                                                                             |
| **LFO FREQUENCY** fader        | Charge/discharge resistor of the LFO's relaxation oscillator (op-amp Schmitt + RC integrator — the standard cheap analog LFO).                                                                          |
| **LFO ∿ / OFF** switch         | Mute switch on the LFO output, or power-gate on the LFO op-amp.                                                                                                                                        |
| **OUTPUT** rotary              | Per-channel volume pot before the channel mix bus.                                                                                                                                                     |
| **SWEEP WIDTH** fader (0..6)   | Pot scaling the **pitch-sweep envelope** output into the VCO CV. 0 = no sweep, 6 = ~6-octave glide.                                                                                                    |
| **SWEEP UP / OFF / DOWN**      | DPDT switch routing the sweep envelope either inverted, off, or non-inverted into the VCO CV. UP = envelope output adds frequency at hit, DOWN = inverted, OFF = bypass.                                |
| **SENSE LEVEL** knob           | Variable threshold/gain at the peak detector — controls velocity sensitivity per channel.                                                                                                              |
| **PULL ▶ Ch_n** (MULTI VCO)    | When the SENSE knob is pulled, a switch closes that **routes the trigger pulse into the next channel's VCO CV** as a fixed extra current, so triggering Ch1 *also* causes Ch4 to fire. This is the "cascade triggering" the search hits described. |
| **BEAT SENSE OFF / ON** (global) | Enables velocity-tracking on the *amplitude envelope* peak voltage. OFF = every hit is the same loudness; ON = peak detector output modulates the envelope's start voltage.                                |
| **MASTER VOLUME**              | Output bus gain stage (op-amp summing amp with feedback pot).                                                                                                                                          |
| **POWER / PHONES**             | Bias + headphone amp.                                                                                                                                                                                  |

A handful of these blocks are confirmed by the public Pollard Syndrum
schematic; the rest are educated inferences from the panel and from
the era's standard practice (op-amp + RC + OTA-VCA, all on JFET
input op-amps because that's what Japanese designers reached for in
1978).

## 4. What's different on the DS-4 vs the Pollard 478

Reading the DS-4 panel against the Pollard Syndrum 478 service-manual
description, four blocks are *added* by Toyo Gakki:

1. **OCTAVE 1..5 selector** — Pollard has only a TUNING pot.
2. **Per-channel LFO with LEVEL + FREQUENCY + on/off** — Pollard
   Syndrum has no LFO; cyclic modulation has to come from manual
   tuning fiddling. Putting an LFO into each channel is a real
   architectural change.
3. **MULTI VCO PULL cascade** — Pollard channels are independent.
4. **BEAT SENSE on/off** — Pollard always tracks velocity.

So in DSP terms, the DS-4 is "a Pollard Syndrum with **LFO,
cascade-trigger, and selectable velocity-tracking** strapped on".

## 5. Confidence levels

* **High confidence** (from public sources):
  * Two-envelope-generator architecture per channel.
  * ICL8038 (or pin-compatible equivalent) as the VCO core.
  * Trigger-input conditioning with peak detector + Schmitt.
  * Per-channel band-passed noise mixer.
* **Medium confidence** (from the panel + era convention):
  * OTA-pair VCA (CA3080 / LM13700 / BA662). 1978 Japanese drum synths
    pretty much all used these.
  * Op-amp relaxation oscillator for the LFO.
  * 5-step current divider for the OCTAVE selector.
* **Low confidence** (inferred — *needs verification against the
  service manual*):
  * Exact PCB layout, op-amp models (most likely TL072 / 4558).
  * Noise source: probably a reverse-biased 2SC1815 BE junction, but
    could be a 4006/4011-based pseudo-random shift register on some
    revisions.
  * Whether the 8038's sine output is actually used or whether the DS-4
    runs the triangle through a separate softclip shaper.
